Western Electric (WECO) Rules

Western Electric (WECO) rules are a standard method used in Statistical Process Control to determine whether a process is behaving normally or showing signs of special-cause variation that may require attention.

Rather than reacting to every data fluctuation, WECO rules help engineers and manufacturing teams distinguish between normal process noise and meaningful signals that suggest instability, drift, or loss of control. These rules are widely used across manufacturing environments including semiconductors, optics, aerospace, automotive, and other production settings.

  • Rule 1: One point outside the 3-sigma control limit
  • Rule 2: Two out of three consecutive points beyond 2 sigma on the same side of the mean
  • Rule 3: Four out of five consecutive points beyond 1 sigma on the same side of the mean
  • Rule 4: Nine or more consecutive points on one side of the mean
  • Rule 5: Six or more points in a row increasing or decreasing
  • Rule 6: Fourteen or more points alternating up and down

These rules support consistent decision-making by identifying patterns that may indicate the process should be investigated, adjusted, or left alone.

Control Charts & Process Monitoring

Control charts are one of the core tools of SPC. They track process performance over time relative to a process average and statistically derived control limits.

When used correctly, control charts help teams monitor process stability, identify unusual behavior early, and avoid unnecessary adjustments to processes that are operating normally.

  • Visualizes process behavior over time
  • Uses center line, warning limits, and control limits
  • Highlights out-of-control points and abnormal patterns
  • Supports engineering review and reaction planning

Automated Alerts & Engineering Response

SPC becomes more valuable when signals are connected to timely action. Automated alerts can notify engineers or manufacturing teams when warning or control limits are exceeded so that issues are reviewed quickly and consistently.

  • Supports faster awareness of emerging process issues
  • Reduces dependence on delayed manual review
  • Improves consistency in engineering response
  • Can be integrated into normal review and escalation workflows

Process Capability

In addition to process stability, SPC is often used together with process capability analysis to evaluate how well a process can meet defined requirements or specifications.

Capability metrics help determine whether a process is not only stable, but also capable of consistently producing acceptable results.

  • Cp: Potential capability relative to specification width
  • Cpk: Actual capability accounting for process centering
  • Pp: Overall process performance relative to specifications
  • Ppk: Overall performance including centering effects

Capability analysis is typically most meaningful when the process is already reasonably stable and the measurement approach is understood.

Why SPC Matters in Manufacturing

SPC provides a structured, data-based approach for determining when a process should be investigated, adjusted, or left unchanged. This helps reduce overreaction to normal variation while improving responsiveness to meaningful process shifts.

  • Improves process visibility
  • Supports earlier detection of drift and instability
  • Promotes more consistent engineering decisions
  • Strengthens process control and manufacturing discipline
  • Supports quality improvement, troubleshooting, and RCCA
  • Can be integrated into MES, dashboards, and recurring engineering reviews
