Program Execution, Task Assignment & Tracking

A major part of my implementation leadership is converting a roadmap into assigned, scheduled, and traceable work. I do not limit project leadership to reporting status; I help define the technical activities, determine who must participate, establish sequencing and dependencies, and follow the work through testing, release, ownership transfer, or closure.

  • Break implementation roadmaps into workstreams, activities, and deliverables
  • Assign owners and coordinate responsibilities across technical and operational teams
  • Establish priorities, target dates, sequencing, dependencies, and readiness gates
  • Use Microsoft Planner and structured action trackers to manage tasks and schedules
  • Lead recurring implementation meetings and maintain visibility of open decisions
  • Track configuration, work instructions, testing, training, release, and sustainment activities
  • Escalate technical or organizational blockers and drive issues toward accountable closure
  • Adjust plans when manufacturing requirements, resources, or system constraints change

This combination of planning, task control, technical decision support, and follow-through is a consistent part of how I lead Manufacturing Execution System implementations and other engineering programs.

Technical Development Leadership

I have also led development work where the objective was to evaluate a new technical approach rather than deploy an established process. As the technical lead contact for the oxide coating team, I led an Internal Research and Development (IRAD) effort evaluating Diamond-Like Carbon (DLC) and oxide-based durable-coating approaches. The work investigated how to improve durability while limiting the optical-transmission loss associated with highly durable coating solutions.

  • Defined the performance problem and development objective
  • Led technical direction and evaluation of alternative coating architectures
  • Planned experimental development and characterization activities
  • Balanced competing durability and optical-transmission requirements
  • Coordinated technical input across oxide, infrared, equipment, metrology, and manufacturing stakeholders

This project remained in development when I left the organization and had not yet reached production implementation.
