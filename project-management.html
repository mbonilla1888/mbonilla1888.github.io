How I Run Program Execution

I convert program objectives into assigned, budgeted, scheduled, and traceable work. That requires more than reporting status: I help define the technical activities, determine who must participate, align resources and procurement, establish sequencing and dependencies, and carry the work through testing, release, ownership transfer, or closure.

  • Break implementation roadmaps into workstreams, activities, and deliverables
  • Assign owners and coordinate responsibilities across technical and operational teams
  • Establish priorities, target dates, sequencing, dependencies, and readiness gates
  • Forecast labor demand and compare planned work with available or budgeted hours
  • Develop budget inputs and remaining-work estimates for future execution periods
  • Coordinate parts, material orders, procurement actions, and supplier decisions
  • Use Microsoft Planner and structured action trackers to manage tasks and schedules
  • Lead recurring implementation meetings and maintain visibility of open decisions
  • Track configuration, work instructions, testing, training, release, and sustainment activities
  • Escalate technical or organizational blockers and drive issues toward accountable closure
  • Adjust plans when manufacturing requirements, resources, or system constraints change

This operating model connects people, technical decisions, cost, schedule, procurement, and delivery instead of managing each as an isolated activity.

Situation & Role

Situation: A manufacturing department needed a controlled digital execution system after a substantial software and implementation investment had not produced a sustainable production solution.

Role: I assumed direct ownership of the department implementation and served as its primary MES technical authority, implementation lead, administrator, and manufacturing subject-matter expert.

Execution & Outcome

Outcome: Brought the platform into production in approximately three months and established a sustainable MES capability supporting approximately 200 users, digital execution, governance, analytics, monitoring, and continued operational use.

Ongoing Business Impact

As the solution gained broader enterprise interest, headquarters assumed the estimated $150K annual base licensing cost. This created approximately $150K per year in cost avoidance for the local Microelectronics department, while expanded-site licensing remains a separate enterprise cost.
