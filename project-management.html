Technical Project & Program Leadership

I lead complex technical initiatives where successful execution requires project structure, manufacturing understanding, systems thinking, and the ability to coordinate decisions across functions.

Across manufacturing and engineering roles, I have repeatedly been asked to turn loosely defined technical needs into roadmaps, workstreams, ownership, deployment plans, and sustainable operating practices. My strongest leadership work occurs where program execution and technical depth must operate together.

Program Structure

Convert technical objectives into roadmaps, workstreams, milestones, dependencies, priorities, ownership, and executable activities.

Cross-Functional Execution

Coordinate manufacturing, engineering, quality, maintenance, Information Technology, vendors, operators, and leadership toward a common operational objective.

Technical Authority

Understand the process, equipment, data, or manufacturing system well enough to identify readiness gaps, challenge assumptions, and support sound technical decisions.

Department MES Transformation

Situation & Role

Situation: A manufacturing department needed a controlled digital execution system after earlier implementation efforts had not produced a sustainable production solution.

Role: I assumed direct ownership of the department implementation and served as its primary MES technical authority, implementation lead, administrator, and manufacturing subject-matter expert.

Execution & Outcome

Execution: Built the implementation roadmap, organized workstreams, assessed manufacturing readiness, defined system structures, coordinated cross-functional decisions, and led configuration, testing, training, production release, and sustainment.

Outcome: Deployed a sustainable department MES capability supporting approximately 200 users, digital production execution, governance, analytics, monitoring, and continued operational use.

Implementation Roadmap

  • Platform and governance foundation
  • Asset management and site infrastructure
  • Process structure and virtual manufacturing modeling
  • Production control and digital execution
  • Quality systems and Statistical Process Control
  • Operational intelligence, automation, training, and scaling

Manufacturing Improvement Leadership

Performance Management Team

At Lockheed Martin, I led a Performance Management Team effort focused on capturing operator concerns and driving issues toward accountable closure.

I created a dashboard covering process, safety, material, equipment, work-area, and production barriers; coordinated ownership and follow-up; and maintained visibility from identification through closure. The effort received a Performance Management Team Advocate Award.

Optical Manufacturing Recovery

As a coating-process and equipment owner, I coordinated technical work across process development, equipment, inspection, operations, and quality to address recurring manufacturing failures.

The combined improvement effort reduced failures from multiple per week to near-zero within months and helped enable selected production deliveries as much as one month ahead of schedule.

Facilities Workload Model

Developed a workload and resource model that converted distributed facilities responsibilities into visible demand, ownership, and staffing needs. The analysis supported approval and hiring of dedicated personnel.

Equipment Capability Expansion

Supported manufacturing capability expansion through equipment troubleshooting, upgrades, vendor coordination, procurement evaluation, installation and qualification planning, and transition into sustainable production support.

Technical Development Leadership

I have also led development work where the objective was to evaluate a new technical approach rather than deploy an established process. One example was an Internal Research and Development effort investigating oxide-based alternatives to Diamond-Like Carbon durable optical coatings.

  • Defined the performance problem and development objective
  • Led technical direction and evaluation of alternative coating architectures
  • Planned experimental development and characterization activities
  • Balanced competing durability and optical-transmission requirements

This project remained in development when I left the organization and had not yet reached production implementation.

How I Lead Technical Initiatives

My project-leadership experience has developed through repeated responsibility for technical initiatives—not through a claim of continuous formal program-management tenure. The consistent pattern is being trusted to create structure, coordinate the right contributors, understand the technical work, and keep execution moving.

Define the Work

Clarify objectives, scope, requirements, ownership, dependencies, readiness gaps, and success criteria.

Organize Execution

Create roadmaps and workstreams, sequence activities, coordinate decisions, and maintain action visibility.

Connect Technical & Operational Needs

Translate between manufacturing, engineering, quality, equipment, data, Information Technology, and leadership.

Transition to Sustainable Use

Plan testing, training, release, governance, support, ownership transfer, and continuous improvement.

Leadership Through Technical Depth & Execution

My strongest leadership roles occur where technical complexity and organizational coordination overlap. I can structure the work, communicate across functions, manage dependencies, and understand the underlying engineering well enough to recognize when an issue requires more than project-status reporting.

That combination allows me to move initiatives from loosely defined technical problems toward controlled execution, deployment, and sustained operational use.
