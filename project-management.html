Technical Project & Program Leadership

I lead technical initiatives where successful execution requires both project structure and enough subject-matter expertise to understand the work being performed.

My role frequently combines implementation leadership with hands-on technical ownership: defining roadmaps, organizing workstreams, identifying gaps and dependencies, coordinating cross-functional teams, supporting technical decisions, and driving initiatives toward deployment or operational closure.

Structure & Planning

Transform broad technical objectives into roadmaps, workstreams, milestones, dependencies, priorities, and executable tasks.

Cross-Functional Execution

Coordinate engineering, manufacturing, quality, maintenance, IT, vendors, operators, and leadership toward a common objective.

Technical Leadership

Serve as a subject-matter expert while leading the work, allowing technical gaps and implementation risks to be identified early.

How I Lead Technical Programs

My project leadership experience developed through engineering and manufacturing roles where complex work needed someone to create structure, coordinate the right people, and keep technical execution moving.

I do not approach project leadership as schedule tracking alone. I typically understand the manufacturing process, equipment, data, quality requirements, or technical system well enough to identify what is missing and help the team resolve it.

  • Define project objectives and implementation scope
  • Create roadmaps and structured workstreams
  • Break complex initiatives into executable activities
  • Establish priorities, sequencing, milestones, and dependencies
  • Identify operational and technical readiness gaps
  • Coordinate cross-functional meetings and technical decisions
  • Track open actions and drive issues toward closure
  • Plan testing, training, release, and transition to operations
  • Provide technical leadership when execution requires subject-matter expertise

MES Implementation Leadership

MES implementation has become one of the clearest examples of my project and program leadership because the work crosses manufacturing, engineering, quality, equipment, IT, vendors, training, and operations.

I independently led a department MES implementation from initial planning through production deployment, while also serving as the technical MES and manufacturing subject-matter expert.

  • Created implementation roadmaps and project workstreams
  • Assessed department readiness for digital manufacturing
  • Defined process, equipment, product, quality, and data requirements
  • Coordinated manufacturing, engineering, quality, maintenance, IT, and vendors
  • Sequenced configuration, testing, training, and deployment activities
  • Identified missing processes and ownership before digitization
  • Led production-release planning and post-deployment support

My MES responsibilities later expanded into cross-site and enterprise implementation work, including leadership activities involving an internally developed enterprise MES solution at another manufacturing location.

I also currently support a broader digital-manufacturing organization working on enterprise MES and machine-integration efforts. Because that assignment is relatively recent, I treat it as an expanding leadership responsibility rather than a completed implementation example.

Manufacturing Improvement Leadership — Performance Management Team

At Lockheed Martin, I led a Performance Management Team (PMT) effort focused on capturing operator concerns, improving manufacturing conditions, and ensuring reported issues were assigned and driven to closure.

I created a dashboard to organize and track issues raised by operators. The topics were broader than engineering alone and included manufacturing processes, safety, materials, work-area conditions, equipment needs, and other barriers affecting daily production.

  • Collected and organized operator feedback
  • Created dashboard-based visibility of open actions
  • Prioritized manufacturing and operational concerns
  • Coordinated owners and follow-up actions
  • Tracked items from identification through closure
  • Supported recognition of employees who identified meaningful improvements
  • Worked across engineering, manufacturing, operations, and support functions

This effort received a PMT award and demonstrated a leadership model centered on visibility, accountability, employee engagement, and consistent follow-through.

Technical Development & IRAD Leadership

I have also led technical development work where the objective was not simply to execute an established manufacturing process, but to evaluate a new technical approach.

One example was an Internal Research and Development (IRAD) effort investigating alternatives to Diamond-Like Carbon (DLC) durable optical coatings. DLC provides strong durability but can reduce optical transmission, so the project evaluated oxide-based approaches intended to improve the balance between durability and optical performance.

  • Led the technical direction of the development effort
  • Defined the performance problem and development objective
  • Evaluated alternative coating architectures
  • Planned experimental development and characterization activities
  • Balanced competing durability and optical-performance requirements

The project remained in development when I left the organization and had not yet reached production implementation.

Early Technical Project Execution

My comfort with complex technical projects began during graduate research. Early responsibilities included safely disassembling laboratory equipment for relocation and later supporting installation, startup, and troubleshooting of replacement research systems in the same laboratory space.

I did not design those systems; the research faculty and equipment vendors defined the equipment. My responsibility was the hands-on execution required to safely remove, install, operate, maintain, and troubleshoot the tools.

That experience helped establish the practical equipment and execution skills that later became valuable when leading manufacturing projects involving complex tools, processes, vendors, and production requirements.

Leadership Through Technical Depth & Execution

My strongest project-leadership roles occur where technical complexity and organizational coordination overlap. I can structure the work, communicate across functions, track dependencies and milestones, and also understand the underlying engineering well enough to recognize when an issue requires more than project-status reporting.

That combination allows me to lead initiatives from loosely defined technical problems toward structured execution, deployment, and sustained operational use.
