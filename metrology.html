I use metrology to connect process conditions with material, device, optical, and manufacturing performance.

My characterization experience spans electrical measurements, spectroscopy, atomic-scale microscopy, optical metrology, physical-property measurements, environmental testing, and durability evaluation — supported by hands-on experience operating, troubleshooting, maintaining, and calibrating advanced measurement systems.

Metrology & Characterization Experience

My metrology experience dates to 2008, beginning as a user of electrical, microscopy, and spectroscopy techniques and progressing into advanced surface-science characterization, atomic-scale measurements, shared-equipment ownership, and industrial manufacturing metrology.

Over time, my role expanded from collecting measurements to becoming responsible for understanding the instruments themselves — operating, maintaining, troubleshooting, calibrating, and restoring equipment while also interpreting the underlying physics and connecting the measurement results to process performance.

This combination allows me to approach metrology from three directions: the measurement technique, the measurement equipment, and the engineering problem the data is intended to solve.

Surface Chemistry & Electronic Structure Spectroscopy

I have extensive experience using photoelectron spectroscopy to characterize the chemical composition and electronic structure of surfaces, thin films, and interfaces.

X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)

Elemental composition, core-level analysis, chemical states, oxidation, contamination, chemical shifts, bonding environment, and surface/interface chemistry.

Ultraviolet Photoelectron Spectroscopy (UPS)

Valence-band structure, occupied electronic states, work-function-related measurements, energy levels, and electronic alignment.

Inverse Photoemission Spectroscopy (IPES)

Unoccupied electronic states, conduction-side electronic structure, band-gap determination, and combined occupied / unoccupied energy-level analysis.

Ultra-High-Vacuum Surface Analysis

Surface-sensitive measurements in Ultra-High Vacuum (UHV) environments, including sample preparation, vacuum-system operation, contamination control, and measurement troubleshooting.

Selected spectroscopy example showing XPS, UPS, IPES, UHV instrumentation, electronic-structure measurements, and surface analysis.

Atomic-Scale Imaging & Surface Morphology

I have advanced experience characterizing thin-film surfaces from nanoscale morphology down to atomic-scale structure.

  • Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM)
  • Atomic-resolution surface imaging
  • Scanning Tunneling Spectroscopy (STS)
  • Local current-voltage measurements with atomic-scale spatial resolution
  • Surface morphology and topography
  • Atomic lattice and structural measurements
  • Step-height and nanoscale dimensional measurements
  • Identification of different surface phases and structures
  • Correlation of thin-film growth conditions with resulting surface structure
  • Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)
  • Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)
  • Nanofiber and nanomaterial morphology characterization

This capability is particularly useful for thin-film and semiconductor work because it connects process conditions with what physically formed on the surface rather than relying only on nominal recipe parameters.

Environmental, Durability & Qualification Testing

Metrology in manufacturing is not limited to measuring initial product performance. I have also used environmental and mechanical testing to determine whether coatings remain functional after exposure to expected stresses.

  • Humidity and environmental exposure testing
  • Thermotron environmental chamber ownership
  • Chamber calibration and condition verification
  • Environmental chamber troubleshooting and maintenance
  • Automated scratch-durability testing
  • Scratch-test equipment ownership and support
  • Coating adhesion evaluation
  • Delamination testing
  • Post-exposure optical and physical evaluation
  • Investigation of failures following environmental testing

These methods supported process qualification, durability verification, manufacturing troubleshooting, and Root Cause and Corrective Action (RCCA).

Measurement-System Ownership & Troubleshooting

A significant part of my metrology capability comes from having responsibility for the measurement equipment itself rather than treating metrology as a black-box service.

  • Advanced measurement-system operation
  • Equipment installation and setup
  • Shared-tool ownership and user support
  • Instrument troubleshooting
  • Calibration support and verification
  • Vacuum-system troubleshooting
  • Measurement-method troubleshooting
  • Maintenance and component replacement
  • Recovery of equipment following failures or maintenance
  • Evaluation of abnormal or questionable measurement results
  • Technical coordination with vendors when required

This is especially valuable in process engineering because a questionable result can come from the product, the process, sample preparation, the measurement method, or the instrument. Understanding all of those possibilities reduces the risk of making the wrong manufacturing decision from bad or misunderstood data.

Physics & Measurement Science Foundation

My metrology work is supported by a physics and materials-science background that helps connect instrument output with the physical mechanisms producing the measurement.

Surface science, quantum mechanics, electronic states, condensed-matter physics, optics, materials science, semiconductor physics, and vacuum physics all contribute to how I interpret characterization data.

The objective is not simply to obtain a measurement. It is to understand what the measurement means, whether it is trustworthy, and what engineering action should follow from it.
