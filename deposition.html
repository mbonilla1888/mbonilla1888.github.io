Vacuum Systems & Thin-Film Process Physics

My deposition experience spans pressure regimes from manufacturing vacuum equipment through ultra-high-vacuum research systems operating near 10⁻¹¹ Torr.

Working across these regimes developed a practical understanding of how chamber condition, residual gases, pumping behavior, temperature, materials, and pressure influence both equipment performance and film growth.

  • Vacuum, high-vacuum, and ultra-high-vacuum environments
  • Experience across approximately 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹¹ Torr depending on process
  • Rate-of-rise testing and chamber-integrity evaluation
  • Distinguishing true leaks from outgassing and residual contamination
  • Gas purity and controlled-reactant delivery
  • Pumping behavior and chamber recovery
  • Temperature behavior under reduced-pressure conditions
  • Material and film stress during pressure and temperature transitions
  • Controlled heating, cooling, pump-down, and venting
  • Effects of chamber cleanliness on process stability and film quality

This background is particularly valuable during troubleshooting, because identical pressure readings can result from very different physical conditions such as a leak, water or solvent outgassing, contamination, incomplete pumping, or residual process gases.

Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD)

My industrial semiconductor experience includes Atomic Layer Deposition process engineering at Intel. ALD uses sequential, self-limiting surface reactions to provide highly controlled film thickness and conformality.

The sequence below illustrates a conceptual HfO₂ ALD cycle using alternating precursor exposures and purge steps.

1 — First Precursor Exposure

The first precursor reacts with available surface sites until the surface reaction reaches saturation. Excess precursor and reaction products are then removed through the purge step.

2 — Second Precursor Exposure

After purging, the second precursor reacts with the modified surface and completes the second half-reaction.

3 — Surface-Reaction Cycle Completion

Repeating the precursor-and-purge sequence builds film thickness through controlled, self-limiting surface reactions.
