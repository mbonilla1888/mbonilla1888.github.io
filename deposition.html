Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE)

  • PVD technique for UHV deposition (~1e-09 to 1e-11 Torr).
  • Multi-stage pumping: ion pump, turbo-molecular pump, roughing pump.
  • Highest purity; controlled deposition down to sub-monolayer regime.
  • Max substrate temperature ~500°C; slow rate (few microns/hr).
  • Co-deposition or sequential deposition with anneal.
  • Common MBE tools are not designed for mass production.

Effusion Cell and Valved Cracking Tube

Effusion cell

  • Typically used for III-V MBE materials.
  • Source temperatures up to ~1400°C.
  • Electron emission/acceleration drives sublimation of source atoms (as configured in many sources).

Valved cracking source

  • For high vapor pressure materials (Se, S, Te, As, P).
  • Cracks clusters into atoms; stable flux using 3 temp regions:
  • Bulk/crucible (~100°C), valve (~250°C), hot-wall cracking tube (>500°C).

E-beam Evaporation

  • PVD technique where electrons heat the source material to evaporate it.
  • E-gun generates electrons; magnets focus beam to the target.
  • Rotating crucible supports multiple sources for deposition.
  • Manufacturing-capable for thin-film production.
  • Thickness control is strong, but not like ALD/MBE for atomic-scale control.

Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD)

  • CVD technique depositing sequential layers using precursors + inert carrier gas.
  • Excellent thickness control via self-limiting surface chemistry.
  • Manufacturing-capable for high-quality films.

Example: HfO₂ (conceptual)

  1. Introduce first precursor (e.g., HfCl₄).
  2. Surface reactions proceed until saturation (self-limiting).
  3. Purge with N₂ to remove excess reactants/byproducts.

ALD continued

  1. Introduce second precursor (e.g., H₂O).
  2. Surface reaction removes ligands from the first half-reaction.
  3. Purge again to clear excess species.

ALD cycle completion

  • One full ALD cycle forms ~one layer (depending on chemistry).
  • Repeat cycle to build thickness with angstrom-level control.
  • CVD is generally more conformal than PVD (less line-of-sight dependence).

Plasma Enhanced Chemical Vapor Deposition (PECVD)

  • CVD process using plasma to provide formation energy (lower substrate temperature than thermal CVD).
  • Depending on conditions, substrate can rise to ~200°C.
  • RF power influences density/stress of films.
  • Manufacturing-friendly due to higher rate, higher pressure operation, and plasma cleaning.
  • CVD is generally more conformal than PVD.
