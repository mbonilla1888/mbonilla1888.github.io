American Physical Society Bridge Program

In 2013, I was selected as part of the inaugural cohort of the American Physical Society (APS) Bridge Program, participating during the national launch and early development of the program.

The experience extended well beyond financial support. As part of the first cohort, I participated in APS meetings, conferences, professional development activities, networking events, and direct engagement with APS leadership and the broader physics community.

National Fellowship & Professional Development

APS Bridge Program — Inaugural Fellow

The Bridge Program provided a pathway into doctoral physics education while also creating a national community of students, faculty, mentors, and APS leaders focused on graduate success and expanding participation in physics.

Years later, APS News highlighted my completion of the Ph.D. and professional transition as part of its coverage of Bridge Program graduates.

Recognition, Learning & Knowledge Transfer

The common thread across these experiences is continued growth and the ability to transfer technical knowledge to others.

Competitive fellowships created opportunities to develop as a scientist. Teaching required communicating difficult concepts clearly. Industrial recognition came from applying technical knowledge to practical manufacturing problems. Continued learning has allowed me to move across disciplines without losing technical depth.

Together, these experiences support the leadership approach shown throughout this portfolio: understand the problem deeply, create structure around it, communicate it clearly, and help others execute successfully.
