From Optical Design to Production

Optical coating design was only the starting point. A theoretical layer structure had to be converted into a stable manufacturing process capable of repeatedly meeting the optical requirement.

  • Define optical performance requirements and coating architecture
  • Create and optimize the multilayer thin-film design
  • Translate the design into deposition parameters and equipment recipes
  • Run engineering trials and measure deposited coatings
  • Adjust process parameters based on measured spectral performance
  • Qualify the process and establish repeatable production settings
  • Create manufacturing work instructions for operators
  • Provide first-line engineering support when production issues occur
  • Work with quality to investigate defects and implement corrective actions

Durable Coatings & IRAD Development

I owned manufacturing capability for Diamond-Like Carbon (DLC) durable optical coatings and later led an Internal Research and Development (IRAD) effort investigating alternative durable coating approaches.

DLC provides strong environmental durability, but its optical absorption can reduce transmission. The IRAD effort investigated oxide-based coating approaches intended to achieve a better balance between durability and optical transmission.

  • DLC coating process ownership and manufacturing support
  • Technical ownership of the production DLC chamber
  • Process development and qualification
  • Equipment maintenance, troubleshooting, and upgrades
  • Support for procurement and expansion of additional DLC capability
  • Technical lead for development of alternative durable coatings
  • Evaluation of durability versus transmission tradeoffs

The IRAD effort remained in development when I left the organization and had not yet reached production implementation.
