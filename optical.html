From Optical Design to Production

Developing a production optical coating requires more than creating a theoretical layer stack. The design must be converted into a deposition process that can consistently meet optical and manufacturing requirements.

  • Develop optical requirements and coating architecture
  • Create and optimize the theoretical thin-film design
  • Translate the design into deposition parameters and chamber recipes
  • Run engineering trials and characterize deposited coatings
  • Adjust process parameters based on measured spectral performance
  • Establish repeatable production settings
  • Create manufacturing work instructions for operators
  • Support production as the technical point of contact
  • Investigate defects, excursions, and quality issues
  • Implement corrective actions and process improvements

IRAD — Development of Alternative Durable Optical Coatings

I led an Internal Research and Development (IRAD) effort investigating durable optical coatings that could provide improved environmental resistance while preserving more optical transmission than traditional DLC solutions.

DLC provides excellent durability, but the optical absorption associated with the coating can reduce transmission. The project investigated oxide-based coating approaches intended to improve the balance between durability and optical performance.

  • Technical lead for the coating-development effort
  • Evaluation of alternative durable optical coating architectures
  • Optical design and process-development planning
  • Balance of durability requirements with transmission performance
  • Experimental coating and characterization strategy

The effort was still in development when I left the organization and had not yet reached production implementation.
