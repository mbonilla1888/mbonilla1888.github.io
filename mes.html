MES Program Leadership & Manufacturing Systems Implementation

Manufacturing Execution Systems (MES) are the digital layer used to manage and document how production is executed on the shop floor — what is being built, which process route it follows, what equipment and materials are used, what inspections or approvals are required, and whether the work is ready to move to the next operation.

I lead MES implementations from department-level deployment through cross-site, sector, and enterprise manufacturing initiatives.

My role extends beyond configuring MES software. I assess manufacturing readiness, structure implementation workstreams, coordinate technical and operational teams, translate shop-floor requirements into digital workflows, and lead execution through testing, training, deployment, and sustained operations.

Implementation Leadership

Roadmaps, workstreams, readiness assessment, cross-functional coordination, testing, training, deployment, and sustainment.

Manufacturing Expertise

Converting real manufacturing processes, equipment, quality requirements, product flow, and engineering needs into usable digital execution.

Systems & Integration

MES configuration, data structures, SQL, dashboards, equipment connectivity, interfaces, automation, and operational analytics.

Manufacturing Readiness Before Digitization

One of the most important parts of an MES implementation occurs before system configuration begins. Departments are not always operationally structured in a way that can immediately be translated into a digital system.

I help identify and close those gaps so that MES reflects a controlled, executable manufacturing process rather than digitizing ambiguity.

  • Clarify process ownership and responsibilities
  • Define manufacturing steps and product flow
  • Establish equipment naming, hierarchy, and states
  • Identify required product and lot attributes
  • Define quality, inspection, and approval requirements
  • Establish maintenance and qualification structures
  • Define data ownership and reporting requirements
  • Identify training and governance needs before deployment

Governance, Training & Adoption

A technically correct MES implementation is not successful unless manufacturing personnel can use it consistently and the organization has clear standards for maintaining it.

  • MES work instructions and controlled documentation
  • User and engineering training
  • Role and permission structures
  • Naming and data-entry standards
  • Engineering ownership of manufacturing data
  • Recurring implementation and operational reviews
  • Cross-site support and knowledge transfer
  • Post-deployment issue resolution and continuous improvement

MES as a Manufacturing Leadership Problem

The difficult part of MES is rarely the software alone. Successful implementation requires manufacturing processes that are understood, ownership that is defined, data that is meaningful, teams that are aligned, and a deployment structure capable of moving from planning into sustained production.

My value in MES programs comes from being able to operate on both sides of that problem: leading the implementation while also understanding the manufacturing processes, equipment, data, quality requirements, and technical systems well enough to recognize what is missing and help complete the work.
